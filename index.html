Abstract

Controlling camera motion is essential for video generation, as it offers creators a powerful cinematic tool for shaping visual narratives. However, existing camera-controlled video generation models rely on complex and opaque control formats that are inaccessible to everyday users. In this work, we introduce CamPrompt, a video-to-video generation model that enables natural camera control through a single text prompt. Given a source video, its caption, and a simple text instruction describing the desired camera motion (e.g., “Turn the camera to the right.”), CamPrompt re-shoots the video from a novel camera trajectory specified by the instruction. We annotate a high quality multi-camera video dataset with natural language descriptions of camera movement for training (released below), and design a novel dense prompt conditioning strategy and a text–camera alignment module for effective control. Extensive experiments show that CamPrompt attains strong visual quality and accurate camera control, with a 6.7% decrease in FVD compared to the state-of-the-art approach while generalizing robustly across complex instructions. CamPrompt represents a step toward more user-accessible and intuitive controlled video generation.

Method

CamPrompt builds on a Wan2.1 text-to-video diffusion transformer and conditions on the source video by concatenating source and target video tokens along the frame dimension.

  • Dense camera prompts. Instead of one global camera prompt, the model takes four dense prompts, each describing the camera motion over one quarter of the video. At inference the user still writes a single instruction; an LLM rewriter expands it into the dense format.
  • Dense Aggregated Temporal Attention (DATA). Source and target tokens are split into four temporal chunks, and the i-th chunk cross-attends only to the i-th camera prompt.
  • Simple 6-DoF camera extrinsics. During training, the model also receives the trajectory as six numbers per frame (roll, pitch, yaw, x, y, z). At inference, a second LLM rewriter produces these values from the user's instruction.
  • Text–camera alignment. Camera prompt embeddings are explicitly aligned with the corresponding camera extrinsics so the text encoder better reflects camera-motion semantics.

Dense Aggregated Temporal Attention: each temporal chunk of the source and target video attends to its own camera prompt.

Dataset: Camera-Motion Text for MultiCamVideo

We release the text annotations used to train CamPrompt: a caption and four dense camera prompts for every training video of the MultiCamVideo dataset from ReCamMaster (13.6K Unreal Engine 5 scenes, each rendered by 10 cameras). Each camera trajectory is split into four consecutive segments, the relative 6-DoF motion of each segment is computed from the dataset's camera extrinsics, and Gemini 2.5 Flash turns it into a short instruction; captions are also generated with Gemini 2.5 Flash. The annotations are keyed by video path, so they join directly to the original dataset. Videos are not included; download them from the original dataset.

We also release a single camera prompt per video, which summarizes the whole trajectory in one instruction. It was not used to train CamPrompt; we provide it for anyone who prefers one prompt per video over four.
